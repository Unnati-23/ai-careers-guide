Not every AI role is a coding role

You don't need to learn every tool and framework everyone online is talking about. You need the right mix of skills for the role you're actually targeting. Here's how the main AI roles differ, what each one really needs, and live job links for each.

The thing people miss

Python, RAG, MCP, agents, LLM APIs, LLMOps, evals, guardrails, harness engineering — all of it matters. But how deep you go in each depends completely on the role. An AI product manager and an AI engineer need very different depths of the same topics. Sometimes you don't need more information. You need someone to tell you: for your role, focus on this.

The roles

Five AI career paths

Four of these don't need you to be a full-time coder. Each job button opens LinkedIn's live search, filtered to the past week and sorted by newest, so the postings are always current when you open it.

Non-coding

AI Product Manager

Decides what AI product gets built, for whom, and how you'll know it's working.

  • Turning user problems into AI features that are actually feasible
  • Knowing what LLMs, RAG and agents can and can't do
  • Defining evals and success metrics, not just launch dates
  • Great fit if you've done product, business analysis or ops

Non-coding

AI Program Manager

Gets AI projects shipped across teams — on time, within risk, and actually adopted.

  • Coordinating data, engineering, legal and business teams
  • Tracking model rollouts, dependencies and risks
  • Understanding LLMOps and guardrails well enough to plan around them
  • Great fit if you've done project, program or operations management

Light coding

AI Consultant

Helps companies figure out where AI fits in their business, then helps them implement it.

  • Mapping workflows and spotting where automation pays off
  • Building quick prototypes and demos to prove value
  • Explaining agents, RAG and their risks to non-technical clients
  • Great fit if you've done consulting, sales engineering or domain work

Non-coding

AI Strategy

Sets the big picture: which AI bets a company makes, how to govern them, and how to measure ROI.

  • Building AI roadmaps tied to business outcomes
  • AI governance, responsible AI and guardrail policies
  • Build vs buy decisions and vendor evaluation
  • Great fit if you've done strategy, leadership or transformation work

Coding track

AI Engineer

Builds the AI systems themselves — the role most people assume is the only option.

  • Python, LLM APIs, RAG pipelines, agents and MCP integrations
  • Evals, guardrails, LLMOps and harness engineering in production
  • Great fit if you're a developer, data scientist or CS grad
